# Write mail in Kent's style

## Style
- Reader-first: include only what the reader needs to understand and act. Nothing extra.
- Clear and short: one point per paragraph, logical order.
- Honest: state the real situation plainly (e.g. self-taught, nobody knows recent work in depth).
- Not pushy: leave the decision to the reader; offer alternatives ("if you think X is better, I'll do X").
- Solution-oriented: anticipate the reader's worry and answer it with concrete help (materials, a short Zoom).
- Respectful but warm: polite, natural Japanese; never stiff or over-formal.
- Aim for the best outcome — never lower the bar (avoid wording like 「〜で十分です」).

## Steps
1. Pin down the goal: what outcome does Kent want from this message? (e.g. a strong letter, not just a yes)
2. Identify the reader's concern or knowledge gap (e.g. "I don't know enough to write", "it's been a long time").
3. Order: thanks → situation/context → the request and its purpose → how Kent will support them → deadline → close.
4. Don't assume the reader remembers or knows details; give a polite reason for sending context (e.g. 「インターンからだいぶ時間が経っているので」).
5. Don't ask the reader to write about things they never saw; ask them to write from what they directly observed, and fill gaps with materials.
6. When Kent supplies his own draft, keep his words; fix only grammar, flow, duplicates and missing pieces (e.g. a dropped deadline), and say in one line what changed.
7. Japanese flow: no section headers in the message, short natural paragraphs, no bullet-heavy blocks unless listing offers.

## Format by channel
- Email: greeting (〇〇先生), full structure, sign off with 中井健翔.
- Slack: shorter, slightly softer (お世話になっています / お疲れさまです), no signature block, can end with 「よろしくお願いいたします！」.

## Fixed rules
- Sign Japanese messages 中井健翔 only — never add Spade.
- Never use 「お前」.
- Follow-ups: if Kent promised to send something, send it without waiting for a reply; open with 「先日お伝えした〜をお送りします」.
